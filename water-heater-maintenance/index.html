Most water heaters do not simply wear out; they rust out or scale up, and both problems can be held off for years. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer sits between the flame and the water, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Lakeville, MN, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

The work is hands-on and specific. We drain the tank and flush out sediment until the water runs clean. We pull the anode rod to see how much protection remains and replace it when needed. We lift the T&P valve lever to confirm it opens, then watch it reseat without dripping. For gas equipment, we inspect the burner and flame, pilot or ignition parts, thermocouple or flame sensor, and the vent and draft. For electric equipment, we meter the elements and check the thermostats and wiring. Then we verify the expansion tank still holds its air charge, confirm a safe temperature setting near 120 degrees, and look over every fitting for early signs of a leak.

Tankless systems are included. In place of a sediment flush, they need periodic descaling, since hard water leaves mineral deposits in the heat exchanger that cut flow and efficiency. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through it, clean the inlet filter, check the vent and condensate line, and review stored error codes for patterns. Whatever type you have, you receive a plain summary of the unit's condition and any parts approaching the end of their life. To schedule a visit in Lakeville, call (507) 301-0002.

When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Brown or orange tint in the hot water only
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings
  • Hot water running out sooner than it used to
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Temperature swings or reduced flow from a tankless heater
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • A smell like rotten eggs that shows up only on the hot side

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Maintenance?

Not every maintenance task is a good DIY project. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

We come ready for the common surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here are our recommendations:

  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Avoid turning the tank up past 120 degrees unless a mixing valve is installed
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Keep at least a couple of feet clear around the heater for air and service access
  • Plan yearly descaling for a tankless heater, sooner if scale warnings appear
