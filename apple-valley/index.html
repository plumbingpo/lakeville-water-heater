Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Apple Valley

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, lift the T&P valve lever once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year if their water is hard.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing symptoms like rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one with water on the floor.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Take a spent anode rod: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a flooded utility room.

Common Water Heater Problems in Apple Valley

A water heater in Apple Valley almost never fails without warning. Pay attention to a popping or rumbling sound while the tank heats, rust-colored hot water, a faint rotten-egg smell, a damp spot under the tank, or showers that run cold sooner every month. These are early signs of heavy sediment, a depleted anode, a weak element or burner, and early corrosion, and caught early, they're usually an affordable repair or a planned replacement. Ignored, they lead to a failed tank and water on the floor. Pay close attention to a relief valve that drips or discharges: it's the tank telling you pressure or temperature is running too high, and it should be checked promptly. We diagnose the cause, not just the symptom, so the small problem never gets the chance to grow.
