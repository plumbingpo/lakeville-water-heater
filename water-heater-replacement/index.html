A tank past eight to twelve years has done most of its work, and the serial number on the label tells us exactly how old yours is. The symptoms come next. Water leaking from the tank, rust showing up only in the hot water, and heavy sediment noise all say the tank is close to finished. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Energy bills rising while hot water gets harder to come by
  • A tank at or past the eight-to-twelve-year mark
  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot side only
  • Water seeping out from under the tank jacket
  • Loud rumbling or popping that flushing no longer fixes
  • Repairs that keep coming back, one part after another

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Replacement

A water heater swap seems easy right up until you count the connections. Gas units need a leak-free connection with a sediment trap, a correctly sized and pitched vent that sends carbon monoxide outdoors, and enough combustion air. An electric unit needs a circuit and breaker matched to the element wattage. Each tank needs a properly rated relief valve whose discharge pipe ends somewhere safe, and many homes also need an expansion tank, a drain pan, or seismic straps to meet local code. Going tankless or switching to a heat pump adds even more: bigger gas lines, different vent materials, dedicated circuits, and condensate drains.

A professional replacement handles every one of those details once. Our experienced technicians size the heater to your household, verify the gas line, vent, and electrical service, and install whatever code items apply. We leak-test, check draft and carbon monoxide on gas units, and set a safe temperature before we leave. It is what separates a heater that runs quietly for years from one that has problems from the start. And if a repair would buy you several good years on your current tank, we tell you that honestly instead of selling you a new one.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Flush the new tank once or twice a year to keep sediment from building up
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Note the install date and keep up with maintenance so the next replacement is never an emergency
  • Have the anode rod checked around year three to five and replaced if it is worn
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safety and slower mineral buildup
