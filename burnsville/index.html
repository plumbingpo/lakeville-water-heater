Keeping Your Burnsville Water Heater Running Longer

Many premature tank failures start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Drain and flush the tank every year to clear sediment, get the anode rod checked every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Keep the temperature near 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year so the heat exchanger stays clean.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Burnsville Homeowners Face

Most water heater problems we see in Burnsville start with what's in the water. Depending on whether a home is on municipal water or a private well, minerals can be part of the picture: calcium and magnesium settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, and that's why regular descaling is worth keeping up with in hard-water areas.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and near the end of that window the anode rod has often been used up, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. Homes here also run on a mix of fuel types, gas tanks in some houses and electric tanks in others, so our technicians handle everything from failed thermocouples and draft problems to burned-out elements and failed thermostats.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well may see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless heater on hard water needs annual descaling. And an older install getting a new heater often needs updates it never had: an expansion tank, a correctly piped relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are much cheaper to address early. If your water heater is doing anything unusual, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one. Call (507) 301-0002 and we'll take a look.
