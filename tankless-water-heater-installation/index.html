Why do so many people switch to tankless? Mostly three things: hot water that keeps coming, lower standby energy use, and space. A tankless heater stays idle until you open a tap, then fires a high-output burner or electric element to heat water as it flows. With no stored water to keep warm, it wastes less energy between uses, and with regular descaling it can last two decades. It is not magic, though. Performance depends entirely on sizing and installation, which is where our attention goes on every Lakeville, MN install.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We size by flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver noticeably less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which often means a larger gas line or confirming the meter can keep up. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need well over 100 amps, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

Every tankless install from Lakeville Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, proper gas piping with a drip leg, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is what shortens tankless life most. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Why Professional Tankless Water Heater Installation Matters

The equipment is only half the job. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so the size of the gas line, the capacity of the meter, and the pressure at high fire all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are set by the manual and the code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Miss one of these, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

Our technicians handle the full picture. We size the unit by flow rate and winter temperature rise instead of guessing, verify gas supply at full fire, and install venting and condensate lines exactly to specification. Every install gets isolation valves for easy descaling and a properly piped relief valve. We also give honest advice when tankless is not the best fit. For some households, a high-efficiency tank or a heat pump water heater makes more sense, and you will hear that from us before you spend money on a system that does not match how you live.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Here's where to begin:

  • Treating hard water protects the heat exchanger from mineral buildup
  • Keep the inlet screen clear so flow stays strong and the unit fires reliably
  • Avoid cranking the temperature higher than you need; about 120F works for most homes
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • Plan on yearly descaling through the service valves, sooner if your water is hard
  • If the unit shows a recurring code, note it and have it checked
