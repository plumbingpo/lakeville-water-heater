Upfront Pricing

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Lakeville Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing on every job, covering everything from a burned-out heating element to a full tank replacement. Every visit begins with a careful inspection of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a plain-English recommendation with the price spelled out before any work starts.

Many water heater problems stay hidden until a failure is well underway. Sediment builds up on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod slowly corrodes away, and the tank lining starts to rust from the inside with no visible warning. If you notice rusty hot water, popping or rumbling sounds, a rotten-egg smell, shorter hot showers than you're used to, or moisture around the base, call for an inspection right away. Catching it early often keeps a fixable issue from turning into a flooded floor.

Not every visit is about a breakdown. Plenty of homeowners call us to evaluate an aging heater before it fails, and it's a smart move. With a typical tank lifespan of 8 to 12 years (tankless units often run 20 years with annual descaling), a replacement on your own timeline beats scrambling for a new heater with water on the floor. We'll give you a straight answer on how much life your equipment has left.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

The best repair is the one you never need. That's the whole point of our maintenance plans. On each scheduled visit, we flush the sediment out of the tank, check the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the elements and wiring, and descale tankless heat exchangers, all matched to the heater in your home.

Many homes do best with a yearly flush, and houses on hard water often need two. We adjust the schedule to the age of your equipment, how hard your household works it, and local water quality, which determines how quickly sediment accumulates and how long an anode rod lasts.

The savings add up faster than most people expect. A tank without a sediment crust transfers heat with less effort, a fresh anode rod keeps corrosion off the tank walls, and a tested relief valve is ready to do its job if pressure ever climbs too high. Compared with the cost of replacing a tank early, regular maintenance is money well spent.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, the number of gallons of hot water it can deliver in a busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold when the incoming water is cold. That's why we never just copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, the number of bathrooms, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water demands. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely keeps up once kids are showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver steady hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: installed price, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical service, and the footprint, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Frequent Failures

The Water Heater Repairs Local Homes Need Most

The majority of service calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The hot water runs out fast, the tank pops every time the burner fires, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Rusty water, a rotten-egg smell, and a dripping relief valve round out the list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is a warning that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. If you ever smell gas near the heater, leave the house and call your gas utility from outside before you call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on fittings, drain valves, and relief lines.
